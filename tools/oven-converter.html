Your oven recipe, as a starting point for an air fryer

Kitchen Counter · An arithmetic convention, not a tested conversion

Air fryers move hot air fast in a small space, so the usual advice is to set them lower and check earlier than an oven recipe suggests. That advice is a rule of thumb people repeat, not a measurement anybody here has taken. The tool applies it honestly, names it, and clamps the answer to the temperature range the listings on this site actually publish.

Typical oven settings run from about 300°F to 450°F. Try 400 to see the shape of the answer.

Why this is only a starting point

  • The load changes the answer. A single layer of frozen fries and a piled basket of raw chicken need different times at the same setting.
  • The basket changes the answer. The listings on this site range from two 3-quart baskets to a single 26-quart oven cavity. Those do not behave alike.
  • The setting is not the temperature. What the dial says and what the food experiences are different things, and confirming the second one means measuring it.

If you want the setting that actually matters — the internal temperature of the food — use the cook-to-temperature card instead. That table comes from the USDA, and unlike this converter it is not a rule of thumb.

Not a food-safety tool. Nothing here tells you whether food is safe to eat. Safety comes from reaching a minimum internal temperature, which you confirm with a thermometer.
