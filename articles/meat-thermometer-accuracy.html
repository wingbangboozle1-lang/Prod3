What ±0.5°F actually means (and what NIST certification covers)

Kitchen Counter · 2026-11-19 · 4 min read · Specs from official listings

The ThermoMaven listing claims "±0.5°F accuracy" with "NIST & NSF Certified" and a 1-second read. Here is how to read that claim — and its limits.

What ±0.5°F means

It means the displayed temperature is claimed to be within half a degree of the true temperature. At a 145°F target, that is the difference between "done" and "not done" being a rounding error rather than a guess. It is a manufacturer claim printed on the listing; we quote it, we do not independently test it.

What NIST certification covers

NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology) traceability means the sensor was calibrated against a national standard. The listing includes a calibration certificate in the box. It does not mean NIST tested your unit after a year of grilling — calibration drifts, which is why the certificate matters.

Why read time matters as much

A 1-second read means you check often instead of guessing. A thermometer you use ten times beats a more accurate one you use once. The CHEF iQ Sense answers this differently: continuous wireless monitoring with 5 sensors.

Read every accuracy claim this way: number (±) + standard (NIST/NSF) + read time + certificate in the box. If any element is missing, the claim is weaker.
Is ±0.5°F better than ±1°F?

Yes, on paper — half the error band. In practice, probe placement (thickest part, away from bone) matters more than the last half-degree.
