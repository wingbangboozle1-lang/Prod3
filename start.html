Start here · Four kinds of cook

What kind of cook are you buying for?

Five tools, four short paths. Pick the description that sounds like your week — or the week of the person you are buying for — and you land on the one shelf that matters, not on a wall of flags.

Each path is two or three clicks to an official Amazon listing. Nothing here is a test result, and no prices are shown: the listing is the only place a current price is real.

Two things at once

“I cook two different things, and they need to finish together.”

Start with: the dual-basket air fryer. Two independent baskets with a sync-finish feature.

Trade-off: each basket is smaller than one big basket. Check the sizes before you decide “more baskets” means “more food”.

Air fryer shelf · the three air fryers compared on layout, capacity and footprint
Bake and roast

“I want an oven on the counter, not just a fryer.”

Start with: the French-door countertop oven — a wide temperature range and the biggest cavity of the five.

Trade-off: it is the largest unit here by every listed dimension, and the doors need room to open. Measure first.

Oven or toaster oven? · the footprint comparison
A quick check

“I stand at the grill and want the number now.”

Start with: a handheld instant-read. One probe, read on the spot, nothing to charge but a battery.

Trade-off: accuracy and speed figures are the seller's claims, not measurements. Read what the claims do and do not mean.

Thermometer shelf · what ±0.5°F does and does not promise
Walk away

“I want to leave it in and watch from the sofa.”

Start with: a wireless probe system — three leave-in probes and a hub, read in an app.

Trade-off: more parts to charge and clean, and the readings depend on a phone. The listing disagrees with itself on battery life and warranty.
