The specification question

A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Four checks that turn a capacity claim into something you can compare: the unit, the split, the shape, and the number the listing does not give you.

Check four things: whether the number is quarts or litres and whether the listing is consistent about it, whether the capacity is split into drawers or held in one cavity, whether the shape can hold the food you actually cook, and what the usable basket depth is — which most listings never state.

5 min read · specification-led · sources S03 S04 S05

Check one: the unit, and whether the listing agrees with itself

Capacity appears in two places on an Amazon listing: in the title and bullets, which marketing writes, and in the specification field, which is structured. They disagree more often than you would expect.

The Chefman dual-basket listing says 6 liters in the field and 6 quarts in the title. Six US quarts converts to about 5.68 litres, so the field is not a conversion of the title — one of them is simply wrong, and the listing does not say which.

The Emeril oven says 26 quarts in the title and 24.6 liters in the field, and that pair does agree. When the two agree, you can move on. When they do not, treat the number as nominal and nothing more.

Check two: split or single

Two 3 qt drawers and one 6 qt basket are both described as six quarts and are not interchangeable. A drawer has to be able to hold the item on its own. A whole chicken that fits a 6 qt basket does not fit a 3 qt drawer.

Ask what the largest single item you cook is. If the answer is a whole bird or a 12 in pizza, neither dual-basket machine here will do it and the oven will: its listing states it rotisseries a 6 lb chicken and bakes a 12 in pizza.

Check three: shape, not just volume

The Chefman TurboFry 8 Qt is 10 in tall. Eight quarts in a 10 in tall body is a wide, shallow volume, which is excellent for a layer of fries and awkward for anything standing up.

Height is listed in the product dimensions for all three air fryers. Read it with the capacity, not instead of it.

Check four: the number the listing leaves out

Usable basket depth — the internal measurement from the floor of the basket to the top of the rim — is almost never published. It is the number that decides whether a small chicken fits.

This site does not estimate it. Where a listing omits a measurement, the site leaves it blank and says so. Blank is a fair answer to a spec sheet that does not have the number.

The recommendation

Before comparing two machines, put their capacity into the same unit yourself and write down the split and the height next to it. If a listing disagrees with itself on the unit, assume neither figure is precise.

It changes if: If you only ever cook one layer of food at a time, capacity is close to irrelevant and counter depth should decide the purchase instead — see the basket-or-oven guide.
