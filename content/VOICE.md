# Voice brief

Written before the guides, so the guides have something to be wrong against. If a page breaks one of
these rules, it is a bug — open an issue or fix it.

## Who is speaking

The **Shopwise Kitchen Desk**. Not a person, and it says so. There is no invented author with a
headshot and a favourite cast-iron pan, because inventing one is the fastest way to make a site read
as generated.

The desk's only real advantage over the listing page is that it has read the specification sheet
carefully and will tell you which number decides the purchase. That is the entire personality.

## The four rules

1. **Answer first.** The first paragraph of a guide answers the question in the headline. Not
   "buying an air fryer can be a daunting task" — the answer, then the reasoning.
2. **Every number has a source.** If a figure appears, it came off a listing on a stated date or out
   of a USDA table. If it did not, it does not appear. A blank with a flag beats a guess with a
   sentence around it.
3. **No experience we have not had.** Nothing on this site has been unboxed. That means no "in our
   testing", no "we found", no "after three months", no durability claims, no "feels sturdy". The
   site says this plainly on the about page and again on every product page.
4. **Recommend, then stop.** Every guide ends in one clear recommendation and the condition under
   which the recommendation changes. No "it depends" paragraphs that never land.

## Banned constructions

These are the tells. If one of them appears, rewrite the sentence.

- "In today's fast-paced world", "when it comes to", "look no further", "the perfect blend of".
- "Unlock", "elevate", "game-changer", "dive in", "navigate the landscape", "seamless",
  "revolutionise", "nestled in".
- "Not only X but also Y."
- A three-item list where the third item is "and more".
- Em-dash-heavy sentences used as a rhythm crutch.
- Hedging stacks: "may potentially help you to possibly".
- Fake specificity: "many users report", "experts agree", "studies show" without a study.
- Exclamation marks in body copy. One per site is already too many.

## Sentence shape

Short, then medium. Vary it. Say the number. Name the trade-off. Do not soften a real drawback with
a cheerful clause immediately after it: if a machine needs 17 inches of counter, say 17 inches and
let that be the end of the paragraph.

## On the products

Three air fryers, two thermometers. The site's claim is not that these are the best on Amazon — it
has no way to know that, and saying so would be the exact overbroad product claim the Associates
Program Policies prohibit. The claim is narrower and checkable: **these are five specific listings,
here is what their specification sheets say on a stated date, here is which one fits which cook.**

## On the competition

Every site in this niche has a "best air fryers 2026" listicle assembled from listings. This site
does not, because it cannot differentiate inside that format. It differentiates on the two things a
listing page will not do: tell you which measurement decides your purchase, and tell you what the
listing itself gets wrong.
