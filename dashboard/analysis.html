Counter-space analysis: quarts per square inch of counter

Nominal capacity divided by counter footprint for the three air fryers, showing the oven-style machine packs about 2.6 times the capacity per square inch — and why that is not a ranking.

The one piece of analysis here that no listing supplies: how much nominal capacity each appliance packs into each square inch of counter. It is arithmetic on published dimensions, not a measurement, and it is labelled as arithmetic.

The method

For each appliance with a published footprint, multiply the listed depth by the listed width in inches to get counter area, then divide the listed nominal capacity by that area. The result is quarts of nominal capacity per square inch of counter. Every input is copied from the product's own listing on 2026-11-19; no input is estimated.

What the numbers show

  • The oven-style machine (P2) delivers about 2.6 times the nominal capacity per square inch of counter that P1 does, and about 2.1 times what P3 does. That is the arithmetic behind the intuition that an oven-style air fryer uses counter space efficiently: it is a box, and a box fills its footprint.
  • P1 and P3 are within 22 percent of each other on capacity per square inch, despite P3 holding two quarts more nominal capacity. P1's two-drawer design spreads the same kind of volume across a wider, shallower body.
  • Depth, not area, is the number that usually decides the purchase. P2 needs 16.9 in of front-to-back counter depth; P3 needs 12.75 in. On a standard 24 in deep counter, P2 leaves about 7 in behind it and P1 about 10.6 in.

Why this is not a ranking

  • Nominal capacity is the manufacturer's headline figure, not a measured usable volume. A basket and an oven of the same nominal capacity do not hold the same food.
  • Counter area is the footprint as listed. It excludes clearance for opening a door, pulling a basket out, or a wall behind the unit. Real clearance needs are larger, and the oven-style machine needs far more front clearance than a basket drawer.
  • Two of the five products have no published dimensions and are excluded rather than estimated (F-04).
  • A higher number is not better. It means more nominal capacity per inch of counter, which may mean a taller unit, not a better one.
