How to use the dimensions

Listed exterior dimensions help screen for fit; they do not state the clearance needed around a running appliance or its usable cooking area. Measure the actual counter and check the maker's instructions for required clearance. Quart labels are not serving-size claims; the Chefman dual-basket listing specifies two separate 3-qt baskets. Review the capacity checklist and space guide before choosing.

Counter space: how much room you get for the cooking space

This is the one calculation on this site that no listing hands you. Take the listed depth and width, multiply them for the counter area, then divide the listed capacity by that area. Every input is copied from the product’s own official listing — read 2026-11-19, and re-checked against the same listings on 2026-11-20 — so you can redo it with a calculator. It is arithmetic on published figures. It is not a test, and it is not a score: a higher number does not mean a better machine, only more nominal capacity for the counter it takes up.

Nominal capacity per square inch of listed counter area. Listed capacity is the maker’s headline figure, not a measured usable volume, and the area is the footprint only — it excludes the room a door needs to open and a basket needs to come out.   • The oven-style machine gives about 2.6 times the nominal capacity per square inch of counter that the 6-quart dual basket does, and about 2.1 times what the 8-quart does. That is the arithmetic behind the intuition that an oven-style air fryer uses counter space efficiently: it is a box, and a box fills its footprint.
  • The two basket-style fryers sit about 22 percent apart on the same measure, even though the 8-quart holds two quarts more nominal capacity. The dual-basket spreads the same kind of volume across a wider, shallower body.
  • Depth, not area, is usually the number that decides the purchase. The Emeril needs 16.9 in of front-to-back counter depth; the 8-quart Chefman needs 12.75 in. On a nominal 24 in deep counter — a common North American base-cabinet depth quoted as a reference point, not a measured standard for any particular kitchen — the Emeril leaves about 7.1 in behind it and the dual basket about 10.6 in.
