Thanksgiving leftovers: what belongs in an air fryer the next day?

Kitchen Counter desk · Published 2026-11-20 · Figures checked against USDA FSIS the same day · Nobody here has reheated anything in these machines

This is not a recipe page. Nobody at Kitchen Counter has used the air fryers we list, so we cannot tell you that a basket makes turkey crispier than a microwave, and we will not pretend otherwise. What we can do is put the two numbers that actually decide the day in front of you, then show what each listing publishes about whether the machine you own can reach them.

The number that decides it: 165°F

USDA’s Food Safety and Inspection Service publishes one minimum internal temperature for everything on this page. Its safe-temperature chart lists Leftovers: 165 degrees F (73.9 degrees C) and Casseroles: 165 degrees F, the same figure it gives for all poultry, with no rest time attached. See the USDA FSIS safe minimum internal temperature chart.

Two consequences follow, and they matter more than any timing table: the centre has to reach 165°F where you measure it, and a reheated casserole or a stack of sliced turkey can be hot in one spot and cold three centimetres away. FSIS is blunt about the measuring part: “Using a food thermometer is the only reliable way to ensure that foods have been cooked to a safe minimum internal temperature.” That sentence is from its Food Thermometers factsheet, which also warns that colour and appearance are not safety signals.

The number that decides whether you still can: two hours

The same factsheet lists as a risk factor “eating perishable foods that sat out at temperatures between 40 degrees F and 140 degrees F for more than 2 hours.” The 40–140°F span is where leftovers sit on a counter while people keep talking. If the pie has been out since the second course, the appliance is the least of your decisions.

What the three listings here actually publish

Reheating gently is a low-temperature problem, and low temperature is a specification, so this is where the listings can be read against each other. The 26-quart oven is the only one whose listing starts low enough for the question to be worth asking.

What nobody is telling you, and we are not either

  • Minutes and temperatures per food. A reheating table is a test result. We have not tested these appliances, and a listing does not publish one for leftovers.
  • “Crisps it back to life.” That is a texture claim about food we have not cooked.
  • Serving sizes from quarts. Quarts are volume, not food-contact area, and a basket’s quoted capacity tells you nothing about whether a slice lies flat. A single layer is the assumption behind even heating; you can check that with a baking pan, not a number.
  • Safety of food that was already mishandled. Reheating does not undo hours in the 40–140°F span.

The checklist that fits on a fridge door

  1. Leftovers into the fridge while the table is still being cleared. The two-hour span is the one FSIS names; the chart’s own advice is to refrigerate leftovers promptly.
  2. Measure the centre, not the surface. FSIS: thickest part, away from bone, fat and gristle; for thin foods, insert through the side to the centre. If the unit has a dimple on the stem, that dimple marks the sensing point — check the maker’s instructions.
  3. Stir anything saucy halfway and measure again. Hot air and microwave energy are both uneven; a stir is the cheap fix.
  4. 165°F at the display, in the middle of the thickest portion, before it goes back on the table.
Is an air fryer safer or better than a microwave for leftovers?

Neither, on the evidence here. The safety question is whether the centre reaches 165°F, and both appliances can do that or fail to do it. We have not tested either, so this page makes no comparison of outcome.

Can I reheat a whole slice of turkey from the fridge straight to 165°F?

The listing tells you the machine can be set to a temperature; it does not tell you how long your fridge, your slice and your basket take. Measure with a thermometer, which is exactly what USDA says a thermometer is for.
