SG-01 implemented effort S medium

Narrow the site to one question: air fryer, or thermometer — not both

Five products across two families read as a list of things someone had links for, not a publication with a beat.

Evidence. The set divides 3 air fryers / 2 thermometers across four brands (F-08). Google's thin-affiliation policy specifically targets affiliate pages whose only reason to exist is the links (S12).

Paths.

  • A. Keep both, and make the beat "cooking to a target temperature" — the air fryer gets food hot, the thermometer proves it is done. This is what the site currently does.
  • B. Narrow to air fryers, and treat the thermometers as one comparison article.
  • C. Split into two sites later, if either earns enough traffic to justify it.

Recommended. A for now. It is the only framing in which both product families belong in the same sentence, and it gives every guide an obvious place to link back to. Revisit at the first Search Console data drop.

Decision owner: owner · Blocks: nothing — the site is live on option A

SG-02 implemented effort S high

Publish the two spec conflicts instead of picking a winner

P1's capacity is stated as both 6 liters and 6 quarts; P2's preset count is both 24 and 25.

Evidence. Program Policies 2(b) prohibits inaccurate or misleading product claims (S02). Choosing one number at random is exactly that.

Paths.

  • A. Show both readings and name the conflict. A buyer learns the listing is sloppy, which is useful.
  • B. Publish the headline number only and say nothing.
  • C. Leave the field blank entirely.

Recommended. A. It costs one sentence and it is the only option that is both accurate and useful. B is the option every competitor picks, which is the reason to not pick it.

Decision owner: editorial · Blocks: nothing

SG-03 implemented effort S high

Publish no prices, anywhere, and say why on a dedicated page

Affiliate sites show prices to feel current. Amazon's policies restrict that sharply.

Evidence. Program Policies 2(b): price and availability may be shown only if Amazon serves the data or it comes through the Product Advertising / Creators API (S02).

Paths.

  • A. Show no prices. Link out. Explain the rule on a page so readers do not think the site is hiding something.
  • B. Join the Product Advertising API and display live prices with the required attribution.
  • C. Scrape and show prices, refreshing often.

Recommended. A now, B when the account qualifies. C violates the policy the site is built to respect.

Decision owner: owner · Blocks: nothing

SG-04 implemented effort M high

Answer eight buyer questions, not eight product reviews

Product pages answer "what is it"; buyers arrive with "which one, and will it fit".

Evidence. People-first content guidance asks whether a page provides insight beyond the obvious and a descriptive, non-exaggerated heading (S14). A product question has a real answer; a review of an untouched product does not (S08).

Paths.

  • A. Eight question-shaped guides, each answerable from published specifications and USDA targets.
  • B. Five long product reviews written without handling the products.
  • C. One long "best air fryers 2026" listicle.

Recommended. A. C is the format that reads as generated, because every site in the niche already has it and none of them can differentiate inside it.

Decision owner: editorial · Blocks: nothing

SG-05 implemented effort S high

Build the compliance gate into the build, not into a checklist

Disclosure placement and rel="sponsored" are the two things that are easy to forget on page fourteen.

Evidence. Agreement §5 makes a disclosure failure a material breach (S01). Google requires rel="sponsored" on paid links (S11).

Paths.

  • A. Generate every page from one template that always emits the disclosure strip, and run a checker that fails on any Amazon link without rel="sponsored".
  • B. A written checklist someone reads before publishing.

Recommended. A. A checklist is a promise; a failing build is a fact. The checker is in tools/check_site.py and runs in CI.

Decision owner: engineering · Blocks: nothing

SG-06 proposed effort M low

Buy one air fryer and one thermometer, and earn the right to say "we used it"

The highest-value content in this niche — what the thing is like to live with — cannot be written honestly without the thing.

Evidence. FTC: "You can't talk about your experience with a product you haven't tried" (S08). Google's E-E-A-T guidance rewards demonstrated experience (S14).

Paths.

  • A. Buy one unit per family out of the commission budget and document a fixed set of tests: heat-up time, evenness with a thermocouple grid, basket clean-up time, counter footprint.
  • B. Stay specification-only, and be the site that is scrupulous about it.
  • C. Accept review units only if the site discloses the gift.

Recommended. B until the site earns anything, then A. A is the only durable moat in this niche, but it should be funded by the site, not by a credit card.

Decision owner: owner · Blocks: the honest writing of any "what it is like to live with" page

SG-07 proposed effort S high

Turn on Search Console before writing guide nine

The next topic is currently chosen by instinct.

Evidence. No Search Console property exists (F-17). Choosing topics from measured queries is the only way to avoid writing eight more articles for nobody.

Paths.

  • A. Verify the property, wait two weeks, and pick the next three topics from the Queries report.
  • B. Keep writing on instinct and check the reports later.

Recommended. A. It costs one DNS or file verification and it changes the entire content plan from guessing to reading. The site already carries the metadata needed for indexing.

Decision owner: owner · Blocks: every content decision after guide eight

SG-08 implemented effort S high

Add a working outbound-click counter that stores nothing about the reader

There is no way to tell whether a guide is read or merely visited.

Evidence. Program Policies 2(b) forbids tying a sub-tag or ID to an individual user (S02). So the counter must be aggregate by construction, not by policy.

Paths.

  • A. Client-side counter: one integer per product in localStorage, no identifier, no network call. Honest but per-browser.
  • B. A self-hosted endpoint that logs a path and a product id and discards the IP.
  • C. A third-party analytics script.

Recommended. A is shipped because it touches nothing and cannot leak. B is the upgrade once there is a host to run it on — it is the only option that gives a real denominator. C is the option that turns a small site into a privacy policy.

Decision owner: engineering · Blocks: nothing

SG-09 implemented effort S high

Give every page a unique title and description, and test that the build rejects duplicates

Duplicate titles and descriptions are the most common self-inflicted indexing problem for generated sites.

Evidence. Google: "Create unique descriptions for each page on your site"; identical descriptions on every page are named as a problem (S13).

Paths.

  • A. Generate each page's title and description from its own content, and fail the build on a duplicate.
  • B. One site-wide description.

Recommended. A. B is the specific failure mode the guidance names.

Decision owner: engineering · Blocks: nothing

SG-10 implemented effort M medium

Make the front page a publication front page, and keep the dashboards one click away in the footer

The verification machinery is genuinely interesting to the owner and irrelevant to a buyer.

Evidence. Page-experience guidance (S14); the reader came for a question, not an audit trail.

Paths.

  • A. Front page leads with the products and the guides; the register, gate, ledger and flagged pages sit in a single footer group.
  • B. Front page leads with the source register, because that is what makes this site different.
  • C. Two sites.

Recommended. A. The audit trail is the site's credibility, not its pitch. It should be exhaustively present and never in the way.

Decision owner: design · Blocks: nothing

SG-11 implemented effort S high

Use typographic hero art and labelled placeholders, and never a generated photograph of a product

The site needs to look published; it has no rights to product photography.

Evidence. The Operating Agreement treats images as Program Content (S01). A generated image of a product is a fabricated claim about what the buyer will receive.

Paths.

  • A. Typographic hero, illustrated placeholders that clearly are not photographs, and a one-line note saying so.
  • B. Generate photorealistic images of the products.
  • C. Take the listing's own images and host them.

Recommended. A. B is fabrication and C is a rights problem the operating agreement does not solve for you.

Decision owner: design · Blocks: nothing

SG-12 implemented effort M medium

Put a three-question chooser above the comparison table

A comparison table asks the reader to hold eleven columns in their head and decide.

Evidence. The three air fryers differ along three axes that are decidable from the listing: form (basket vs oven), capacity, and counter depth. Nothing else materially separates them (S03, S04, S05).

Paths.

  • A. Three questions, each with two or three answers, resolving to one product with a stated reason.
  • B. A filterable table.
  • C. Nothing — let the table speak.

Recommended. A. It is the only one of the three that produces a recommendation rather than a dataset.

Decision owner: design · Blocks: nothing

SG-13 proposed effort S high

Test one short link inside the enrolled Associates workflow before trusting the format

The five supplied links use link.amazon, and their status in the enrolled workflow cannot be settled from outside the account.

Evidence. Program Policies 2(b) prohibits a shortener used so the destination is unclear (S02). The site publishes direct amazon.com/dp/ destinations so clarity is not in question, but the short links' own status is unresolved (F-13).

Paths.

  • A. Click one link while signed in, confirm the destination ASIN and confirm the click appears in the Associates report, then repeat for the rest.
  • B. Switch every link to direct /dp/ URLs and drop the short form.
  • C. Declare the short links non-compliant and remove them.

Recommended. A for the short form as provenance, with the site continuing to publish direct URLs either way. C is a claim the evidence does not support.

Decision owner: owner · Blocks: confidence in attribution on the supplied links

SG-14 implemented effort S high

Start the campaign ledger empty, with defined columns, and fill it only from first-party reports

Every affiliate dashboard invites a fabricated conversion rate on day one.

Evidence. No first-party performance data exists (F-10). A number without provenance is worse than a blank.

Paths.

  • A. An empty ledger with fixed columns — timestamp, source, channel, eligible sessions, clicks, reported orders, provenance — filled only after launch, from the Associates report.
  • B. Estimated figures marked as estimates.

Recommended. A. B ages badly: the estimate outlives the label.
