A no-hype checklist for comparing air fryer capacity

Kitchen Counter · 2026-11-19 · 5 min read · Specs from official listings

"Capacity" on an air fryer listing can mean quarts, liters, or a marketing number. Run this checklist against any listing — we ran it against our three verified air fryers.

The checklist

  1. Convert units. The Emeril lists "26QT" in the title and "24.6 liters" in Product Information. 26 US quarts ≈ 24.6 liters — consistent. Always convert before comparing.
  2. Count the baskets. The Chefman 6 Qt is two 3-qt baskets. Total capacity is 6 qt, but each basket holds 3 qt. Two foods, half the space each.
  3. Run the whole-chicken test. The Emeril listing claims a 6-lb chicken and a 12-inch pizza fit. The basket fryers make no such claim — assume they do not.
  4. Divide by depth. Capacity per inch of counter depth: Emeril 1.54 qt/in, Chefman 8 Qt 0.63 qt/in, Chefman dual 0.45 qt/in.
  5. Check for self-contradiction. The Emeril title says 24 presets; "About this item" says 25. We flag it rather than pick one (FLG-002).
The takeaway: quarts are comparable, adjectives are not. If a listing gives you a number, convert it, divide it by depth, and check whether the listing agrees with itself.
Is 6 quarts enough for a family of four?

For one food at a time, likely yes; for two foods at once, each basket holds 3 quarts. The listing does not publish a serving-size claim, so we do not either.

Why do some listings use liters?

Amazon's Product Information normalizes to liters (24.6 L for the Emeril). The title uses quarts. Both describe the same cavity.
