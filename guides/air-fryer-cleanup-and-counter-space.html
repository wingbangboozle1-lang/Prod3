The daily-cost question

How to assess cleanup and counter-space trade-offs

The specification sheet tells you what is dishwasher safe and what the footprint is. It does not tell you how many parts you will wash. Here is how to work that out before buying.

Count the parts that touch food, check whether each one is listed as dishwasher safe, and measure the counter against the body depth plus the travel of whatever opens. Those three facts are all on the listing; everything else about cleanup is a guess until you own it.

5 min read · specification-led · sources S03 S04 S05

Count the washable parts, not the features

A basket fryer gives you a basket and usually a crisper plate — two pieces. An oven-style machine gives you a rack, a basket, a rotisserie spit, and however many accessories the box includes; the Emeril listing states six cooking accessories are included.

Six accessories is six things that may need washing, and a larger cavity that needs wiping. That is not a reason to avoid an oven; it is a reason to not be surprised by one.

Check the dishwasher claim part by part

Both Chefman listings describe the basket as nonstick and dishwasher safe. The Emeril listing, as captured, does not state a dishwasher-safe claim in the specifications this site recorded, so no value is published against it here.

"Dishwasher safe" usually refers to specific parts, not the appliance. Read which parts before you rely on it.

Measure the counter for the thing that moves, not the thing that sits

A basket drawer pulls straight out and needs roughly its own depth again in front of the machine. A French door swings outward and needs room on both sides as well as in front.

The listed depths are 13.38 in, 12.75 in and 16.9 in. On a 24 in counter, the first two leave 10.6 in and 11.3 in in front, which is tighter than a full drawer travel. This is the trade-off that only shows up in the first week of ownership, and it can be computed before purchase.

Plan for grease, which no listing quantifies

Air frying produces spatter and odour. No listing on this site publishes a filter specification or an odour rating, so this site publishes nothing about either.

The practical mitigation is placement: near a hob extractor if you have one. You can decide that before you buy, and it belongs in the same decision as counter depth.

The recommendation

If you will not hand-wash more than two pieces, the dual-basket Chefman is the least work of the three. If you will, and you have the counter, the oven gives you far more cooking for a similar amount of wiping.

It changes if: If the machine will live in a cupboard rather than on the counter, footprint stops mattering and weight and handle design start to — neither of which is published on these listings.
