The buying question

Basket or oven-style air fryer: what should you compare?

Basket and oven-style air fryers cook differently and fit differently. Compare the four things that actually separate them, with the numbers from three specific listings.

Compare four things, in this order: how much food you cook at once, how much counter depth you have, whether you need two foods to finish together, and whether you want a drawer you can shake or a door you can see through. Capacity and wattage look like the headline numbers and are the least useful of the four.

6 min read · specification-led · sources S03 S04 S05

1. Counter depth, because it is the only requirement that cannot be worked around

An air fryer that does not fit on your counter does not get used, and this is the first number that disqualifies a machine. The three air fryers on this site publish these depths: 13.38 in for the Chefman dual-basket, 12.75 in for the Chefman TurboFry 8 Qt, and 16.9 in for the Emeril Lagasse 26QT oven.

Those figures are the body only. Add clearance for a drawer pulling out, or a French door swinging open, and the footprint you need is larger than the footprint listed. On a nominal 24 in deep counter, the oven leaves 7.1 in behind it before you have opened anything.

This is the least glamorous measurement on the listing and the one most likely to decide whether the purchase was a good idea.

2. How many foods, and whether they need to finish together

A single-basket fryer cooks one thing at a time, or one mixed batch at one temperature. A dual-basket fryer cooks two things at once, and Sync Finish lines up the two timers so they are ready together. An oven-style air fryer uses racks and a larger cavity, so food goes in at different heights rather than in separate drawers.

These are three genuinely different ways to cook, and the distinction matters more than the quart figure on the box. Six quarts split into two 3 qt drawers holds a different dinner than six quarts in one basket.

3. Capacity, read sceptically

Capacity is where listings are least reliable and most persuasive. The Chefman dual-basket lists its capacity twice, as 6 liters in the specification field and 6 quarts in the title. Those are not the same number: 6 US quarts is about 5.68 litres. Both readings are printed on the page, and this site reproduces both rather than picking the flattering one.

The Emeril oven is the reverse case. Its title says 26 quarts and its field says 24.6 liters, and those two do agree, because 26 US quarts is about 24.6 litres.

Treat every nominal capacity as an upper bound declared by the manufacturer, not a measured usable volume. A whole chicken fits in a 6 qt basket and does not fit in a 3 qt drawer, no matter what the two add up to.

4. What you actually watch while it cooks

A basket needs shaking, and all three air fryers here list a reminder or an equivalent. An oven-style machine with an interior light lets you look without opening the door; the Emeril listing names the light explicitly.

If you cook by eye, that light is worth more than a preset count. If you cook by timer, it is not.

The recommendation

If your counter is 15 in deep or less, buy a basket fryer — the two Chefman models fit and the oven does not. Above that, choose the oven if you regularly cook for four to six people or want a second oven, and the dual-basket machine if you mostly cook two components for two people.

It changes if: The recommendation flips if you cannot spare the clearance in front of the machine for a door to swing, which rules the oven out regardless of counter depth.
