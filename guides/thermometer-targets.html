The reference

What temperature should chicken, pork and leftovers actually reach?

The USDA minimum internal temperatures in one table, with the rest time, plus what to do with a reading that lands just under the line.

Poultry 165 °F, ground meats 160 °F, steaks chops and roasts 145 °F plus a three-minute rest, fish 145 °F. Use a food thermometer and measure before the food leaves the heat.

4 min read · specification-led · sources S10

The table

From the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service's safe minimum internal temperature chart: beef, pork, veal and lamb steaks, chops and roasts — 145 °F (62.8 °C) with a rest of at least three minutes. Ground meats — 160 °F (71.1 °C). All poultry, including ground poultry and stuffing — 165 °F (73.9 °C). Eggs — 160 °F (71.1 °C). Fish and shellfish — 145 °F (62.8 °C). Leftovers and casseroles — 165 °F (73.9 °C).

Reheated fully cooked ham has its own rule on the same page: 140 °F (60 °C) if packaged in a USDA-inspected plant, 165 °F (73.9 °C) otherwise.

Why the rest time is part of the number

The three-minute rest attached to the 145 °F figure is not a serving suggestion. It is part of the safety requirement as the USDA publishes it.

Practically, it also means the temperature will keep moving after you pull the food. Measure at the point the USDA specifies and honour the rest.

If the reading lands just under the line

Put it back. A thermometer reading below the minimum means the food has not reached the minimum, and the fix is more heat, not more time on the counter.

Take the reading in the thickest part and away from bone, which conducts heat differently and can mislead a probe.

What this page does not tell you

It does not tell you how long to cook anything. Time depends on thickness, starting temperature, and the machine, and no table can substitute for a measurement.

It also does not cover every food. The FSIS chart covers the categories above; for anything else, read the chart at the source rather than extrapolating from this page.

The recommendation

Tape the five figures to the inside of a cupboard: 165 poultry, 160 ground, 145 whole cuts plus rest, 145 fish, 165 leftovers. They cover almost every cook in a domestic kitchen.

It changes if: They do not change. The page will be dated and re-checked against the FSIS chart; the chart is the authority, not this page.
