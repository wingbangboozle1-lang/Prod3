Quarts per square inch: the counter-space arithmetic nobody publishes

Divide nominal capacity by counter footprint and the three air fryers separate by more than a factor of two. The method, the sums, and why the answer is not the ranking you expect.

The oven-style machine delivers about 2.6 times the nominal capacity per square inch of counter that the dual-basket machine does, because a box fills its footprint and a drawer does not. But capacity per inch is a density measure, not a quality measure.

5 min read · specification-led · sources S03 S04 S05

The method

Take the listed depth and the listed width, multiply them to get the counter area the appliance occupies, and divide the nominal capacity by that area. Every input comes from the product's own listing on the same day; nothing is measured.

This is arithmetic on published figures. It is not a test, and it says nothing about how well any of them cooks.

The sums

Chefman dual-basket: 13.38 in by 11.81 in is 158.0 square inches; 6 qt divided by 158.0 is 0.038 qt per square inch.

Chefman TurboFry 8 Qt: 12.75 in by 13.5 in is 172.1 square inches; 8 qt divided by 172.1 is 0.046 qt per square inch.

Emeril 26QT oven: 16.9 in by 15.8 in is 267.0 square inches; 26 qt divided by 267.0 is 0.097 qt per square inch.

What the numbers mean, and what they do not

The oven wins by a wide margin, and the reason is geometric. A box uses its full footprint for volume; two drawers with walls, handles and gaps between them do not.

The two basket machines land within about 22 percent of each other, which is close enough that the difference should not decide a purchase. Their real difference is two drawers against one.

None of these numbers is a ranking. Quarts per square inch says how densely a machine packs nominal volume into counter space. A very dense machine may simply be tall, and tall is its own problem — the oven is 14.8 in tall against 10 in for the single-basket fryer.

The number to use instead, if you only remember one

Depth. It is the measurement that fails first in a real kitchen, and it is the one nobody puts in a comparison table.

Listed depths: 12.75 in, 13.38 in, 16.9 in. Against a nominal 24 in counter that leaves 11.3 in, 10.6 in and 7.1 in behind the machine, before you open anything.

The recommendation

Use depth to exclude machines and capacity per square inch only to compare machines that already fit. If two machines both fit your counter and you cook two components at once, pick the dual-basket despite its lower density.

It changes if: If you cook for four or more, the oven's density advantage compounds: you get more usable volume per inch, not just more nominal volume. Below that, the drawers are the better shape.
